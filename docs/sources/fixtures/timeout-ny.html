Time Out Market New York

Time Out Market had one mission when it arrived in New York in 2019: to find the best restaurants and bar talents and gather them all under one roof. We did pretty well with the opening of Time Out Market New York, Brooklyn, as the two-story building right on the edge of the Dumbo waterfront packs a curated selection of 19 eateries, three bars and a fifth-floor rooftop that easily gives one of the best views of the skyline beyond.

The newly minted Manhattan sister, Time Out Market New York, Union Square, follows in its footsteps, as the neighborhood model features seven food vendors, a full-service bar and a backyard patio for eating and imbibing.

1. Yalla Fest

  • Things to do

Picture this: A tableful of Lebanese food, artisans selling their goods around the perimeter of the room, music setting the tone and a host who is treating you like family. That's what you get when you attend Edy Massih's Yalla Fest. The Edy's Grocer owner and cookbook author is hosting his third annual fundraiser for Lebanon on Saturday, November 21 at 837 Broadway (2nd fl.) from noon until 7pm. It will feature a variety of Middle Eastern-owned businesses from all over New York and the Tri-state area selling bites, sips and loads of other goods that will make great holiday gifts.

This isn't your average holiday market—rather it's a celebration of Lebanese culture through food, art, music and retail. Newcomers include Beituti, Nour à Noir, Mystical Calligrams and more join returning vendors Lulu's Crackers, Eden’s and Zeytouneh Shop among others. The price to enter is $25, with proceeds being donated to the Lebanese Red Cross, SEAL and Beit el Baraka. Yalla!

2. Two Girls

  • Comedy
  • Until Nov 8, 2026

Two girls. One cup. What could go wrong? Eliya Smith's potentially shocking two-hander, developed with the Goat Exchange and inspired by the viral scat-porn clip that scandalized and titillated the world in 2007, does not try to dramatize the experience of Karla and Latifa, the stars of the Brazilian fetish film Hungry Bitches. Instead, it is drawn verbatim from conversations with men who have watched their scene—which inspired countless reaction videos online—and explores what they remember about it and how much about their own sexuality they are willing to share. Chloe Claudel and Juliana Sass play all the roles in what sounds like both an exercise in docutheater and a subversion of it; Claudel also co-directs it with Mitchell Polonsky.

Audience members must be 18 years or older. See the show at Cherry Lane Theatre through November 29. Tickets range from $30 to $100, depending on the day and seating section.

3. Cozy Fall Movie Series at Foxtail

  • Things to do

One of the best things about fall is that we get to watch the most wonderful, classic, feel-good films with the knowledge that the city outside looks kinda (of not exactly) like the foliage-filled sets. Foxtail cocktail lounge is hosting a cozy movie series every Friday evening (7–10pm) in November at the Living Room at Arlo SoHo. The weekly screenings are not ticketed, but they do require a two-item minimum purchase of food or drinks. Choose between seasonal bites and themed cocktails priced at $18 to $20 or a $19 movie-night special featuring a sandwich, chips and a cookie. 

The November lineup includes Good Will Hunting (Nov 6), When Harry Met Sally (Nov 13), You've Got Mail (Nov 20) and Planes, Trains and Automobiles (Nov 27).

4. Kubrick: A retrospective at MoMI

  • Things to do

The Bronx-born-and-raised Stanley Kubrick gets a retrospective at the Museaum of the Moving Image. From November 1 through December 27, anyone with a penchant for spooky thrills can get their fill at this two-month-long movie marathon. His work is known for being "eccentric and narratively experimental," so if you've been wanting to see what it's all about, now's your chance. The event highlights the legendary director’s full cinematic body of work—which will be shown at the Redstone Theater—and is timed to The Criterion Collection's new box set, The Complete Kubrick.

Ticket prices vary based on elligibility. Adults are $17.50 each, senior and student tickets cost $12, kids are $10 and MoMI members can get in for just $7.

5. The Winter Village at Bryant Park

  • Things to do
  • Markets and fairs

The Bank of America Winter Village at Bryant Park is back in NYC with exciting holiday shops, food and activities running from October 28, 2026, through February 28, 2027.

Its 17,000-square-foot ice-skating rink that’s free to use (if you bring your own skates) is always the highlight, but the Winter Village in all its holiday spirit is a close second. This year, more than 180 new and returning kiosks will be there for you to peruse through until January 3—all at one of NYC's best parks for what's become one of the best Christmas markets in the world!

6. Siena: The Art of Bronze, 1450–1500

  • Things to do
  • Exhibitions

It may be third in the Olympics, but bronze is No. 1 at The Frick Collection. "Siena: The Art of Bronze, 1450–1500" brings together roughly 40 works exploring how Sienese sculptors turned the metal into objects of extraordinary beauty and technical sophistication. The exhibition features masters including Donatello, Vecchietta and Francesco di Giorgio, with many works that are rarely seen outside of Tuscany. Beyond the artistry, the show examines the technical hurdles and political complications that shaped bronze production in 15th-century Siena, revealing the ingenuity behind these intricate creations. It’s a chance to see Renaissance sculpture through a fresh lens and appreciate just how much craftsmanship goes into making something look effortless.

Tickets for The Frick Collection run between $17 (student) and $30 (adult) depending on the ticket you qualify for.

7. Watch: "NAZA"

  • Movies
  • Documentary
  • 5 out of 5 stars
  • Recommended

Some films are important regardless of their cinematic quality. They have an urgency and a relevance that go beyond aesthetic consideration. This new film from Yuval Abraham and Rachel Szor, two of the filmmakers behind Palestinian-Israeli Oscar-winner No Other Land, is one of those. And it’s one of the year’s best.

The film consists of the testimony of 24 anonymous ex-members of the Israeli Defense Force (IDF). The interviews take place in the dark on rooftops in Tel Aviv, overlooking apartment buildings and streets where the night-time traffic passes. Abraham questions his subjects and listens to what they have to say—they are anonymous shapes in the dark with quiet reasonable voices. 

NAZA puts together a restrained, intelligent, articulate, precise and utterly damning case against Israel’s actions in Gaza and their ongoing actions throughout the region. As a work of cinema, it is exemplary. As a work of journalism, it is inarguable.

8. Through Her Lens

  • Things to do

Photography can do more than document a crisis—it can challenge why the world has ignored it for so long. "Through Her Lens," a project from the END Fund and African Women in Photography, brings together five African woman photographers to explore how neglected tropical diseases affect women and girls across the continent. The resulting series moves beyond medical statistics to capture the people, families and communities living with these often-treatable illnesses. Maureen Gathoni, Maheder Haileselassie, Temiloluwa Johnson, Amarachi Nnoli and Jodi Windvogel have each spent time in the communities they photographed, contributing to the project. Their work addresses conditions including trachoma, intestinal parasites, schistosomiasis and lymphatic filariasis. The photographs explore everything from the stigma surrounding illness to barriers to clean water and healthcare, centering the dignity and resilience of those affected rather than reducing them to patients. Explore the outdoor exhibition at Dag Hammarskjöld Plaza through December 3.

9. Watch the NFL's New York Jets and Giants

  • Things to do
  • Sport events
  • 4 out of 5 stars
  • Recommended

Fall means a lot of things, but one of the most universal—in the U.S. anyway—is football. Long ago, New York City was blessed with not one, but two NFL teams: the Jets and the Giants. Unfortunately, the Jets were not blessed with the ability to win (we're still holding onto Super Bowl III), and while the Giants do have four championships under their belt, 2012 was a long time ago in sports years. Still, heading to MetLife Stadium on a Sunday in the fall is a bucket-list experience you'll want to check off—and it's one you may get to have for a lower price than exprected, thanks to those records and the fact that the North East stadium doesn't have a roof (bring a poncho if it's going to rain and a heavy blanket if it's below 35 degrees). If you don't care as much about seeing a good game, and a good time is all you're after, grab some friends and tailgate gear and join the most optimistic pessimists you've ever seen—we're actually a lot of fun.

(Written by a lifelong Jets fan.)
